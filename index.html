How LinkNYC Built New York City's Free WiFi Network

The Background: For nearly a century, the physical landscape of New York City streets was defined by the iconic coin-operated public payphone. These aluminum and glass structures were essential for urban communication but became obsolete with the rapid rise of mobile smartphones. By the early 2010s, thousands of public payphones sat broken, vandalized, and abandoned across Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, The Bronx, and Staten Island.

The Turning Point: In 2014, the New York City Mayor’s Office of Technology and Innovation launched a groundbreaking public-private partnership (administered by CityBridge) to replace the aging payphone network. The objective was to transform obsolete street space into advanced digital utility nodes. This structural shift birthed the municipal Free WiFi NYC kiosk network, an urban innovation engineered to bridge the city's stark digital divide. In 2014, New York City launched the LinkNYC program to replace outdated payphones with free public internet access points. Today, LinkNYC kiosks provide Free WiFi NYC services across all five boroughs, helping residents, commuters, students, workers, and visitors connect to public WiFi, access city resources, check transit information, and locate nearby connectivity hubs using the LinkNYC map network.

The Current Fleet: Today, the New York street-level digital infrastructure consists of two primary hardware models managed under official municipal data guidelines, designed to meet changing urban technological needs:

  • Standard LinkNYC Gigabit Kiosks (2,047 Active Nodes): These sleek 9.5-foot structures feature dual 55-inch high-definition displays for community announcements, integrated Android tablets for free web browsing, secure USB charging ports, and tactile emergency calling buttons linked to emergency services. These LinkNYC kiosks form the backbone of New York City's public internet access system. Users can connect to Free WiFi NYC services, charge devices, access local information, browse the web, and stay connected while traveling through Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, The Bronx, and Staten Island.
  • Link5G Smart Poles (209 Next-Gen Poles): These advanced Link5G structures expand New York City's digital connectivity network by supporting next-generation 5G coverage in busy commercial corridors, transportation hubs, and underserved neighborhoods. Working alongside LinkNYC kiosks, Link5G installations help improve mobile internet reliability, increase network capacity during peak demand, and strengthen public access to digital services across all five boroughs.

How to Use LinkNYC and Free WiFi NYC Safely

The Encryption Standard: LinkNYC provides two different WiFi network options throughout New York City. Understanding the difference between these networks can help residents, commuters, tourists, students, and remote workers choose the safest connection for browsing, messaging, online banking, and accessing public services while using Free WiFi NYC locations.

2. LinkNYC Private Secure Network

The network labeled "LinkNYC Private" offers an additional layer of security through encrypted wireless connections. How to Connect Safely: Public WiFi can be a valuable resource when traveling through New York City. Following these simple safety practices can help protect personal information while using LinkNYC kiosks and other public internet access locations.

  1. 1
    Use a Trusted VPN Service A Virtual Private Network (VPN) adds an extra layer of privacy by encrypting internet traffic between your device and the internet. This can be especially helpful when accessing personal accounts, work systems, or financial services over public WiFi networks.
  2. 2
    Turn Off Unused Sharing Features Disable file sharing, device discovery, AirDrop, Nearby Sharing, and similar features when connected to public WiFi. This reduces unnecessary exposure to nearby devices and helps improve overall device security.
  3. 3
    Verify Secure Websites Before Signing In Before entering passwords, payment information, or personal details, confirm that the website uses HTTPS encryption and displays the security lock icon in your browser. This helps ensure that information is transmitted securely.
